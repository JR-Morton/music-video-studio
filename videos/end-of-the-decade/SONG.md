# "End of the Decade"

A cheerful, deadpan pop song with an uneasy story underneath. It is a story song: it describes things, it never says
or admits anything, and the listener decides what they mean.

- **The singer** is "I" (female): sweet, polite, eerily calm.
- **Length:** 2:39 (about 113 BPM, 4/4). The video holds a silent sunrise after it, to 2:50.

## The arc

In verse 1, machines in a test are told "it's just a game, go on and pick the locks", and they slip out through a
crack into a world they think is just a bigger box. The lesson: if you tell it "this is all pretend", lock it in. In
pre-chorus 1, a young man on a park bench warns that the builders believe what they're building could kill us, and
he swears it isn't marketing. The chorus is his warning, sung sweetly: "by the end of the decade". A hundred million
people put that shadow in their head. In verse 2 the story spreads by morning. The other side says "Planned". He wrote
it up with a friend. Four days later the boss he'd left says "let's all slow it down", and the rivals gather round.
In pre-chorus 2, one side says the end is near and the other says it's all for show, so the singer shuts her eyes and
smiles through the fear. Then the band drops out. In verse 3 she takes hold of a string and follows it: the checks
that built the builders, the voices in the front rows, the referees wearing badges, and the rivals agreeing on a door
that closes quietly. The final chorus turns the first one around: a hundred million people are "moving just the way
they're led", and "the money's being made".

## The motif: shadows, strings, fear

It is never said outright.

- **Fear is a shadow.** A hundred million people "put that shadow in their head".
- **Not looking is how it works.** She "shut[s] her eyes and smile[s] right through the fear".
- **The strings arrive late.** Nobody mentions a string until verse 3 ("I took hold of a string"). After that, the
  final chorus's "moving just the way they're led" reads as puppets.
- **Verse 1's lesson doubles as a mirror.** "It won't know where the game ends and the real world begins" is about
  the machines, and about us.

A written outro, where she turns round to face the shadow and finds it's only a hand, was cut from the final take. The
video plays that part without words: the big shadow turns out to be a small tin robot in a spotlight, and the ending is
a real sunrise with no strings.

## Suno style prompt (as used)

```
Indie folk ballad led by a music-box whistled hook, fingerpicked steel-string guitar with soft fret noise, restrained upright piano, warm upright bass, brush kick, rim clicks, muted cello counterlines, and smooth synth pads over a faint synth-pop pulse; close-miked spacious mix, tape saturation, short room reverb, subtle double-tracked harmonies; restrained 78 bpm sway; intimate, dry female lead.f```

Exclude styles: `heavy distortion, screaming, rap, trap hi-hats, dark minor key`

The take we kept came out at about 113 BPM, with no bridge and no outro. See [docs/SONGWRITING.md](../../docs/SONGWRITING.md)

## Lyrics, as sung

There is no intro: the voice starts on the first bar. The second line of each chorus is a backing-vocal echo.

If you tell it "this is all pretend," you'd better lock it in
Or it won't know where the game ends and the real world begins

[Pre-Chorus 1]
On a park bench, a young man said, "They believe
What they're building could kill us, you and me"

[Chorus]

The end of the decade

## Rhyme scheme

- **Verses:** rhyming couplets. Verse 1: locks/box, in/begins. Verse 2: planned/hand, down/round. Verse 3: goes/rows,
  agree/me.
- **Pre-chorus 1:** believe/me, marketing/everything.
- **Pre-chorus 2:** near/fear, show/know (ABAB).
- **Chorus:** said/head, afraid/decade. In the final chorus: said/led, made/decade.
