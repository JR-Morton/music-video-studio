# "End of the Decade": treatment

The story, cast, motifs and timing, staged in the gl engine's idiom: code-rendered plates, big typography, hairlines,
raymarched 3D, one signal colour, bloom, and the lyric built into every image (never subtitles). **No singer on
screen**: "I" is the type. Where this file and `LOOK.md` disagree, `LOOK.md` wins (it came later and is more
specific: for example, it rules out engraving, guilloché and grain).

## The idea

A cheerful, deadpan explainer of an alarming story. Each section is a plate with its own instrument (a test chamber,
an engraved park, a view counter, a newswire, a split stage, a guilloché cheque...), all sharing one palette, one type
system and one running motif: **the thread.** A single pale hairline runs through every plate doing ordinary jobs: it
draws the box, it's the crack, the reply thread under the post, the newswire, a timeline axis. It is never the subject.
In verse 3 ("I took hold of a string") the camera takes hold of it and follows it up through the clouds, and the
viewer realises every line in the film was a string.

## Palette

- **ink** near-black, **bone** paper and type, **graphite/ash** greys.
- **signal = caution yellow** (~`#FFD21F`): the forced cheer and the hazard tape in one colour. Smiles, the sung word,
  the countdown, the spotlight. It's the only colour that glows.
- **brass** (a dirty gold) belongs to the money: cufflinks, cheques, badges, the final chorus.
- **dawn** (peach/rose light) is used once: the real sunrise at the end. Nothing else is warm and natural in the film.
- Plates alternate **bone paper with ink lines** (forms, blueprints, engravings) and **ink with light** (3D, night),
  which gives the edit its light/dark rhythm.

## Cast, as objects

- **Marionettes** (everyone in the story): artist's wooden lay figures with ball joints, raymarched, lit like
  product shots; strings rise out of frame. They move with the beat-yank: jerked up on the beat, falling back.
- **Tin robots**: 1950s wind-up toys (tin panels, rivets, a key in the back), raymarched with metal shading. They move
  on their own clockwork tick, off the grid, and nobody can get a string on them. Never villains.
- **The hands**: navy pinstripe cuffs and brass cufflinks, only ever from the wrist down.

## Rules

No real people, companies, logos or likenesses. The video never says what anything was; it shows who helped, who
paid, who agreed and which door closed, and leaves the rest to the viewer. The warning side is screen-right and "all for show"
is screen-left. Kind to the robots.

## Review steps

1. This treatment (palette, the thread, the plate list).
2. Style frames: three stills (the `sandbox` chamber with a tin robot, a `bench` marionette, the `decade` hook slam).
3. Model sheets rendered in-engine: tin robot, marionette, the cuffed hands.
4. Plate by plate: stills and contact sheets, then draft clips, before the final render.
